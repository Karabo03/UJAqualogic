The process

From a hidden leak to a technician on site

Four steps, all of them automatic until the moment a person is needed.

01

Sensors watch the line

Pressure is measured at four points along the pipeline, flow is measured going in and coming out, and an ultrasonic sensor tracks the reservoir level.

02

The system finds the zone

A leak shows up as pressure falling between two sensors while more water goes in than comes out. The zone between those two sensors is the one losing water.

03

A technician is assigned

The system picks the next technician on the rotation, shows the operator who is on it, and puts their number one tap away.

04

Everything is written down

Every leak, every report from the public and every dispatch is stored, so there is a record of what happened and who attended.

On the network

What the system keeps an eye on

🌊

Reservoir level

Live tank level with a warning long before the reservoir runs dry, so refills are planned instead of rushed.

📊

Pipeline pressure

Four points along the line. The gap between any two of them is what gives the leak away and points at the zone.

🚰

Water balance

Water entering the line against water leaving it. A gap between the two means water is going somewhere it should not.

📣

Reports from the public

Anyone who sees water in the street can report it. The report lands on the operator's screen straight away.

🔧

Maintenance rotation

A standing team list. Work is shared out evenly, so the same person is not called every single time.

📋

Incident history

A full record of leaks, acknowledgements, dispatches and reports, kept in one place for every operator to see.
